Margin vs markup: the difference that costs businesses money

Clear definitions, the formulas, a conversion table and the common mistake of confusing a 50% markup with a 50% margin.

By the 555558 Editorial Team. Updated October 27, 2026. 5 min read.

Margin and markup both describe profit, but from different starting points. Mixing them up is one of the most common pricing errors in small business, and it always errs on the side of earning less than you planned.

Definitions

  • Markup is profit as a percentage of cost: (price − cost) ÷ cost.
  • Gross margin is profit as a percentage of price: (price − cost) ÷ price.

An item that costs 50 and sells for 100 has a 100% markup and a 50% margin. Same profit, different denominator.

The expensive mistake

An owner wants a 40% margin and adds 40% to cost. Cost 60 becomes price 84, a margin of only 28.6%. To get a true 40% margin, divide cost by 0.6: the price should be 100. On thousands of sales, that gap is the difference between a healthy business and a struggling one.

Then apply an ending

Once the price earns the margin you need, choose the final digits for your market: 99, 8, 88 or round. Make sure the ending doesn't push the price below your target margin.
