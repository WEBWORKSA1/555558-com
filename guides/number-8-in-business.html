The number 8 in business: phone numbers, plates, prices and addresses

How the prosperity meaning of 8 shows up in real transactions, from phone-number auctions to home prices, and how to use it without looking gimmicky.

By the 555558 Editorial Team. Updated October 27, 2026. 7 min read.

Few cultural beliefs have a measurable price tag. The preference for 8 among Chinese-speaking buyers is one of them. It shows up in auction results, real estate listings, product prices and launch dates, which makes it useful knowledge for anyone selling across Asia or to diaspora communities.

Where the meaning comes from

Eight (bā) sounds like the word for prospering (fā). The visual shape helps too: a closed, symmetrical loop that reads as balance and continuity. Its opposite is 4 (sì), which sounds like the word for death and is avoided in floor numbers, prices and phone numbers. Six (liù) suggests things flowing smoothly, and 9 (jiǔ) sounds like "long-lasting", so both are welcome too.

Measurable effects

  • Phone numbers. In 2020 a Beijing court auctioned a mobile number ending in five 8s. It sold for 2.25 million yuan, about US$300,000, after more than 5,000 bids. Businesses pay premiums for lucky numbers because the number is printed on every card and sign.
  • Real estate. US sellers have listed homes at prices such as $888,888 to appeal to Chinese buyers, and an address ending in 888 in Los Angeles was reported to have sold well above similar nearby homes.
  • Licence plates. In Hong Kong, government auctions of plates featuring 8 and 88 regularly draw high bids.
  • Big events. The Beijing 2008 Olympics opened at 8:08 pm on the 8th day of the 8th month.

Using 8 without looking gimmicky

A single 8 in the right place reads as cultural fluency. Eight 8s on a product page reads as a stunt. Good places to start:

  1. Price endings on items aimed at Chinese-speaking shoppers, especially gifts and celebration packages.
  2. Customer service lines: if you can choose your number, a run of 8s at the end costs little and is easy to remember.
  3. Launch dates: the 8th, 18th or 28th of a month, and avoiding the 4th, 14th and 24th for audiences who care.
  4. Quantities and bundles: packs of 8 or 88, red-and-gold packaging around Lunar New Year.

When it backfires

Western audiences mostly don't notice an 8-ending, so it gives up the left-digit benefit of a 9. Buyers who are not superstitious may read heavy use of lucky numbers as marketing theatre. And avoiding 4 entirely can be impossible in phone numbers or addresses you don't control; in those cases, don't draw attention to it.
